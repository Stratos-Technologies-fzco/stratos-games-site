How to play the 29 card game

29 is a classic trick-taking card game from India and South Asia, played by four people in two teams. It rewards memory, teamwork and nerve: you bid how many points your team can win, name the trump suit and then try to keep your promise. This guide explains every rule used in STRATOS 29, plus the strategy that separates casual players from table champions.

The goal of 29

Four players sit around the table. Partners sit opposite each other, so you and the player across from you are one team and the other two players are the other team. The game is played in rounds. In each round there are 28 card points available, and the team that wins the very last trick earns one extra point, which is why the game is called 29.

One player wins the bidding and becomes the declarer. Their team has promised to collect at least as many points as the winning bid. If they do, they score a game point. If they fall short, they lose one. The first team to reach +6 game points wins the match.

Cards and points

29 uses a short deck of 32 cards: the 7, 8, 9, 10, Jack, Queen, King and Ace of each of the four suits (spades, hearts, diamonds and clubs). The order of the cards is not the usual one. From highest to lowest it is Jack, 9, Ace, 10, King, Queen, 8, 7.

Each suit holds 7 points (3 + 2 + 1 + 1), so the whole deck holds 28. Keep this table in mind: the Jacks and Nines are the cards that win games.

Dealing

The dealer gives four cards to each player. After the bidding is finished and trump is chosen, each player receives their remaining four cards, so everyone plays with eight. Because you bid on only four cards, the first deal is where most of the skill and risk lies.

Bidding

Starting with the player after the dealer and moving around the table, each player either bids or passes. The lowest bid is 16 and the highest is 29. Every new bid must be higher than the last. A bid is a promise: "my team will win at least this many of the 29 points." Once you pass you cannot come back in. When everyone else has passed, the highest bidder becomes the declarer. If all four players pass, the cards are thrown in and the next dealer deals again.

How high should I bid? Count your Jacks and Nines in your best suit. A single Jack with a couple of small cards of the same suit is usually worth a bid around 16 or 17. A Jack and a Nine in the same suit, with an Ace or a long suit behind them, can justify 19 to 21. Remember that your partner will bring some points too, but you cannot see their cards, so do not rely on luck.

Choosing trump

The declarer names the trump suit. Cards of the trump suit beat every card of every other suit. Choose the suit where you hold the highest cards and the most of them, because that is where you will be able to win tricks and cut your opponents' valuable cards.

Hidden trump. In the default STRATOS 29 rules the trump suit can stay secret. Only the declarer knows it. The suit is revealed when a player who has no card of the suit that was led asks for it, or when the declarer plays a trump card. A player who asks must play a trump card if they have one. Hidden trump adds bluffing and deduction to the game. You can switch it off in settings if you prefer open trump.

Playing the tricks

The player after the dealer leads any card to the first trick. Going clockwise, everyone plays one card. The rules are simple:

  • You must follow suit: if you hold a card of the suit that was led, you must play one.
  • If you have none, you may play any card. You can discard a low card or play a trump card to win the trick.
  • The trick is won by the highest trump card if any trump was played. Otherwise it is won by the highest card of the suit that was led.
  • The winner collects the four cards (and their points) for their team and leads the next trick.

After eight tricks the round is over and the points are counted.

Scoring

Add up the card points each team captured. The team that won the last trick adds one more. If the declarer's team has at least as many points as the final target, they make their bid and score +1 game point. If not, they are "set" and lose 1 game point. The first team to reach +6 wins the match, and a team that drops to -6 loses it. In STRATOS 29 the game score is shown at all times at the table.

Pair, double and re-double

Pair (marriage)

If you hold both the King and the Queen of trump, you hold a pair. Once your team has won at least one trick you can declare it. For the declarer's team a declared pair lowers the target by 4 points (never below 16). If the opposing team declares a pair, the target rises by 4 (never above 29). Declaring a pair also reveals the trump suit.

Double and re-double

After all eight cards are dealt, the opposing team may double the stakes if they believe the declarer will fail. The declarer's team can answer with a re-double. A doubled round is worth 2 game points and a re-doubled round is worth 4, whether the bid succeeds or fails. Double only when you hold strong cards and the bid looks too ambitious.

Strategy tips

  • Count the Jacks. There are only four. Whoever controls them usually controls the round.
  • Support your partner. When your partner is winning a trick, add a valuable card such as a Ten or Ace if it is safe. Throw away points to your partner, not to your opponents.
  • Do not waste trump. Cutting a trick with no points in it is rarely worth a trump card. Save them for tricks that carry a Jack or a Nine.
  • Track the cards played. Remember which Jacks and Nines are gone. If the Jack of a suit has been played, your 9 is now the top card of that suit.
  • Notice who is out of a suit. A player who discards instead of following suit has none of it left, which tells you when they may cut with a trump.
  • Win the last trick. The extra point decides many rounds that end one point short.
  • Bid for your hand, not your hopes. Opponents who set a high bid collect your game point, and a double makes mistakes even more expensive.

Why are there different rules for 29 in different places?

29 is played all over South Asia and its communities abroad, and families have their own house rules about bidding, pairs and what happens when everyone passes. STRATOS 29 uses one documented standard ruleset and lets you switch hidden trump, double and pair on or off.

What does it mean to be set?

A team is set when it fails to collect the points it bid. It loses a game point, or more if the round was doubled.
